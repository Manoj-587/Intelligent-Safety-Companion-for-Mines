# Conveyor Belt — Fault Diagnosis and Safety

## Common Faults
| Fault | Possible Cause |
|---|---|
| Belt misalignment | Uneven loading, worn idlers, incorrect belt tension |
| Belt slip | Insufficient tension, wet or contaminated drive pulley |
| Overheating | Seized idler, blocked chute causing overload, motor overload |
| Belt damage | Sharp material, incorrect splice, impact from large lumps |
| Abnormal noise | Seized idler rollers, worn bearings, loose structure |

## Inspection Steps
1. **Before starting**: Walk the full length of the conveyor. Check idlers, belt condition, chute clearances, and guards.
2. **During operation**: Monitor belt alignment, listen for seized idlers, check drive motor current (TC862).
3. **After shutdown**: Inspect belt surface for cuts or damage, check idler rotation, inspect drive pulley lagging.

## Safety Precautions
- Never cross a moving conveyor belt — use designated crossings only.
- Never ride on a conveyor belt under any circumstances.
- Isolate and lock out before any maintenance or clearing of blockages.
- Keep the area around the conveyor clear of personnel during start-up.
- Ensure all emergency stop pull-cords are functional and accessible.

## Fire Risk
Conveyor belts are a primary fire risk in underground mines.
- Use only fire-resistant belt material.
- Inspect belt for friction points (seized idlers) that can generate heat.
- Ensure the conveyor fire suppression system is operational.
- If smoke or burning smell is detected, stop the conveyor immediately and raise the alarm.

## When Sensor TC862 is Elevated
An elevated reading on TC862 indicates possible conveyor overload or misalignment.
1. Reduce the feed rate or stop loading.
2. Inspect for belt misalignment and seized idlers.
3. Check drive motor temperature and current.
4. If the reading does not normalise, shut down and notify the maintenance team.
