# Crusher — Fault Diagnosis and Safety

## Common Faults
| Fault | Possible Cause |
|---|---|
| Overload / motor trip | Feed rate too high, oversized material, blockage in crushing chamber |
| Abnormal vibration | Worn or broken jaw plates, loose fasteners, unbalanced flywheel |
| Overheating | Insufficient lubrication, bearing failure, blocked cooling |
| Unusual noise | Foreign object in chamber, worn liners, loose components |
| Reduced throughput | Worn jaw plates, incorrect closed-side setting, feed segregation |

## Inspection Steps
1. **Before starting**: Check lubrication levels, inspect jaw plates for wear, confirm all guards are in place.
2. **During operation**: Monitor motor current (CR863), listen for abnormal noise, check for excessive vibration.
3. **After shutdown**: Inspect jaw plates and liners for wear, check fastener torque, inspect lubrication system.

## Safety Precautions
- **Never** reach into the crushing chamber while the crusher is running or coasting.
- Always isolate and lock out the electrical supply before any maintenance.
- Confirm the flywheel has stopped completely before opening inspection covers.
- Use the correct PPE: hard hat, safety glasses, hearing protection, gloves.
- Never attempt to clear a blockage by hand — use a clearing bar from a safe position.

## When Sensor CR863 is Elevated
An elevated reading on monitor CR863 indicates possible mechanical overload or fault.
1. Reduce the feed rate immediately.
2. Inspect for blockage in the crushing chamber.
3. Check motor current and temperature.
4. If the reading does not normalise, shut down and notify the maintenance team.
