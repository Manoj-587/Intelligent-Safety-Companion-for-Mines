# First Aid — Gas Exposure

## General Principles
- **Do not enter a gas-filled area without breathing apparatus.** A rescuer who collapses becomes a second casualty.
- Move the casualty to fresh air as quickly as possible.
- Call for medical assistance immediately.

## Carbon Monoxide (CO) Poisoning
**Symptoms**: Headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, cherry-red skin (late sign), unconsciousness.

**First Aid Steps**:
1. Move the casualty to fresh air immediately.
2. Loosen tight clothing around the neck and chest.
3. If unconscious but breathing — place in the recovery position.
4. If not breathing — begin rescue breathing (CPR if trained).
5. Keep the casualty warm and still.
6. Seek medical attention immediately — CO poisoning requires oxygen therapy.

## Methane / Oxygen Deficiency
**Symptoms**: Dizziness, rapid breathing, confusion, unconsciousness (no warning smell).

**First Aid Steps**:
1. Move the casualty to fresh air immediately.
2. If unconscious but breathing — place in the recovery position.
3. If not breathing — begin CPR if trained.
4. Seek medical attention immediately.

## Hydrogen Sulphide (H2S) Exposure
**Symptoms**: Eye irritation, coughing, headache, rapid unconsciousness at high concentrations.

**First Aid Steps**:
1. **Do not enter the area without SCBA** — H2S causes instant collapse at high concentrations.
2. Move the casualty to fresh air.
3. Remove contaminated clothing.
4. If not breathing — begin CPR if trained.
5. Seek medical attention immediately — H2S exposure requires hospital treatment.

## Recovery Position
Place an unconscious but breathing casualty on their side:
1. Kneel beside the casualty.
2. Place the nearest arm at a right angle to the body.
3. Bring the far arm across the chest and hold the back of the hand against the near cheek.
4. Pull up the far knee and roll the casualty towards you onto their side.
5. Tilt the head back slightly to keep the airway open.
6. Monitor breathing until medical help arrives.
