# Methane Gas (CH4)

## What is Methane?
Methane (CH4) is a colourless, odourless, and highly flammable gas. It is the primary component of firedamp in underground coal mines. It is lighter than air and accumulates near the roof of mine workings.

## Why is Methane Dangerous?
Methane is dangerous for two reasons:
1. **Explosion risk** — Methane ignites when its concentration in air falls between 5% (Lower Explosive Limit) and 15% (Upper Explosive Limit). Any ignition source — a spark, open flame, or electrical fault — can trigger an explosion.
2. **Asphyxiation risk** — At very high concentrations, methane displaces oxygen and causes suffocation.

## Explosive Limits
- Lower Explosive Limit (LEL): 5% by volume in air
- Upper Explosive Limit (UEL): 15% by volume in air
- Most dangerous concentration: 9.5% (stoichiometric mixture)

## Detection Thresholds (DGMS Guidelines)
- **Warning level**: 0.5% CH4 — increase ventilation, alert supervisor
- **Danger level**: 1.0% CH4 — stop all work, withdraw personnel from the area
- **Evacuation level**: 1.5% CH4 — immediate evacuation of the section

## Sources of Methane in Mines
- Coal seams release methane during mining operations
- Old workings and goaf areas accumulate methane
- Faults and geological disturbances can release sudden methane outbursts

## Immediate Actions When Methane is Detected
1. Stop all ignition sources immediately (machinery, electrical equipment).
2. Increase ventilation to dilute the gas concentration.
3. Alert the supervisor and safety officer.
4. If concentration exceeds 1.0%, withdraw all personnel from the affected section.
5. Do not re-enter until gas levels are confirmed safe by a competent person.

## Prevention
- Maintain adequate ventilation at all times.
- Inspect and test gas monitoring equipment regularly.
- Never use open flames or non-flameproof electrical equipment underground.
