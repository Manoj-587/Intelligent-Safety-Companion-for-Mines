# Mine Gases Overview

Underground mines can contain several hazardous gases. Every mine worker must be able to identify them and know the correct response.

## Carbon Monoxide (CO) — Whitedamp
- Colourless, odourless, tasteless — cannot be detected by smell.
- Produced by: incomplete combustion, fires, blasting, diesel engines.
- **TLV**: 25 ppm (8-hour exposure limit)
- **Danger level**: 200 ppm causes headache; 1600 ppm is immediately dangerous to life.
- Action: Evacuate immediately. Use self-rescuer (CO filter).

## Carbon Dioxide (CO2) — Blackdamp / Chokedamp
- Colourless, slightly acidic smell. Heavier than air — accumulates in low areas.
- Produced by: respiration, oxidation of coal, old workings.
- **Danger level**: Above 5% causes rapid unconsciousness.
- Action: Evacuate low-lying areas. Improve ventilation.

## Hydrogen Sulphide (H2S) — Stinkdamp
- Rotten egg smell at low concentrations. Smell disappears at high concentrations (olfactory fatigue — very dangerous).
- **TLV**: 1 ppm
- **Danger level**: 100 ppm is immediately dangerous to life.
- Action: Evacuate immediately. Do not rely on smell as a warning.

## Nitrogen Oxides (NOx) — Afterdamp
- Produced by blasting operations.
- Causes respiratory damage even at low concentrations.
- Action: Do not re-enter blasted areas until adequate time has elapsed and ventilation has cleared the gases.

## Oxygen Deficiency
- Normal oxygen level in air: 20.9%
- **Warning level**: Below 19.5% — use breathing apparatus.
- **Danger level**: Below 16% — loss of consciousness.
- Caused by: oxidation, gas displacement, old workings.

## General Rules for All Mine Gases
1. Never enter an area with unknown gas conditions without testing.
2. Always carry a personal gas detector.
3. Report any unusual smell or symptoms (headache, dizziness) to the supervisor immediately.
4. Know the location of the nearest self-rescuer station.
