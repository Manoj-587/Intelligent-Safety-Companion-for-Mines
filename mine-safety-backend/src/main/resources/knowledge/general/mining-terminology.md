# Mining Terminology — Glossary

## Common Underground Mining Terms

**Adit**: A horizontal or near-horizontal tunnel driven from the surface into a hillside to access the ore body.

**Afterdamp**: The mixture of gases (mainly CO and CO2) remaining in a mine after an explosion or fire.

**Blackdamp**: Carbon dioxide (CO2) mixed with nitrogen — found in old workings and low-lying areas.

**Drift**: A horizontal underground tunnel driven along or parallel to the ore body.

**Face**: The working end of a tunnel, heading, or stope where mining is actively taking place.

**Firedamp**: Methane gas (CH4) found in coal mines. Highly explosive when mixed with air.

**Goaf / Gob**: The area of a mine from which coal or ore has been extracted and the roof has been allowed to collapse.

**Heading**: A tunnel driven in the direction of the ore body or coal seam.

**Inbye**: Towards the working face (deeper into the mine).

**Outbye**: Away from the working face (towards the surface).

**Pillar**: A block of coal or ore left in place to support the roof.

**Refuge Chamber**: A sealed, pressurised chamber underground where miners can shelter during an emergency. Equipped with air supply, communication, and first aid.

**Rib**: The side wall of an underground tunnel or roadway.

**Self-Rescuer**: A portable breathing device worn by miners that provides protection from CO and oxygen deficiency during evacuation.

**Shaft**: A vertical or near-vertical excavation from the surface used for access, ventilation, or hoisting.

**Stope**: An underground excavation from which ore is extracted.

**Whitedamp**: Carbon monoxide (CO) — produced by fires and explosions underground.

**Winder**: The hoisting machine used to raise and lower the cage or skip in a shaft.

**Working**: Any underground excavation where mining activity is taking place.
