# Mine Ventilation

## Why Ventilation is Critical
Mine ventilation serves three essential purposes:
1. **Dilutes and removes hazardous gases** — methane, CO, CO2, H2S.
2. **Controls dust** — reduces respirable dust concentration.
3. **Controls temperature and humidity** — maintains safe working conditions.

Without adequate ventilation, gas concentrations rise, temperatures increase, and the mine becomes immediately life-threatening.

## How Mine Ventilation Works
Air is circulated through the mine by main surface fans (exhaust or forcing type).
- **Intake airway**: Fresh air enters the mine through the intake shaft or drift.
- **Working faces**: Fresh air reaches the working areas.
- **Return airway**: Contaminated air (with gas and dust) is drawn back to the surface through the return shaft.

## Ventilation Monitoring
- **Barometric pressure sensors** (BA1713, BA1723) monitor pressure differentials that indicate ventilation performance.
- A drop in barometric pressure below the safe threshold indicates potential ventilation issues.
- Airflow velocity is measured at key points using anemometers.

## Auxiliary Ventilation
In development headings and dead-end workings, the main ventilation circuit does not reach the face directly.
- Auxiliary fans and ducting are used to force fresh air to the face.
- The duct must be kept in good condition — leaks reduce airflow to the face significantly.
- The auxiliary fan must run continuously while personnel are working at the face.

## Ventilation Failure — Immediate Actions
1. Stop all work at the affected face immediately.
2. Withdraw personnel from the area.
3. Do not re-enter until ventilation is restored and gas levels are confirmed safe.
4. Report the failure to the supervisor and surface control room.
5. Identify and rectify the cause before resuming work.

## Signs of Inadequate Ventilation
- Gas monitor readings rising above normal.
- Stuffy or stale air at the working face.
- Increased temperature and humidity.
- Dust not being cleared from the face.
- Drop in barometric pressure readings.
